# Articles, `van` and `nincs`, negation, questions, pronouns and address

## The articles
<!-- topics: van-nincs -->
The definite article is `a` before a consonant and `az` before a vowel: `a ház`, `az ablak`
"the window", `az egyetem`. It is used more than in English: with names of languages and
countries in some constructions (`a magyar nyelv`), with abstract nouns, and usually before a
possessed noun (`a könyvem` "my book", `a barátom`). It is not used before a person's name
in the standard language (`Péter`), though colloquial speech often adds it.

The indefinite article is `egy` (also the numeral "one"). It is used less than in English:
`Tanár vagyok.` "I am a teacher." (no article with a profession), `Van kutyám.` "I have a dog."
`Egy` before a noun tends to mean "a certain / one particular".

## `van`, `vannak`, and when the copula is dropped
<!-- topics: van-nincs -->
`van` "is / there is" is the 3rd person singular of `lenni` "to be"; `vannak` is the plural.
In the **present tense, third person**, the copula is **omitted** when the predicate is a noun
or adjective: `Péter tanár.` "Péter is a teacher." `A ház nagy.` "The house is big."
`A gyerekek okosak.` "The children are clever."

`van`/`vannak` **must** appear when the sentence says where something is, that something
exists, or what someone has:

| use | example |
| --- | --- |
| location | `Péter a kertben van.` "Péter is in the garden." |
| existence | `Van itt egy bolt.` "There is a shop here." |
| possession | `Van autóm.` "I have a car." (literally "there is my car") |
| health, state | `Jól vagyok.` "I am well." `Hogy van?` "How are you?" |

In the first and second persons the verb is always present: `Diák vagyok.` "I am a student."
`Otthon vagy?` "Are you at home?" Full present paradigm: `vagyok, vagy, van, vagyunk,
vagytok, vannak`. Past: `voltam, voltál, volt, voltunk, voltatok, voltak`. Future:
`leszek, leszel, lesz, leszünk, lesztek, lesznek` (from `lesz` "becomes / will be").

## `nincs` and `nincsenek`
<!-- topics: van-nincs -->
The negative of `van` is **`nincs`** (never `nem van`); of `vannak`, **`nincsenek`**:
`Nincs időm.` "I have no time." `Péter nincs itthon.` "Péter is not at home."
`Nincsenek gyerekek a parkban.` "There are no children in the park."
With a dropped copula the negation is plain `nem`: `Péter nem tanár.` "Péter is not a teacher."
`A ház nem nagy.` For other persons: `nem vagyok`, `nem vagy`, `nem vagyunk`.

## Negation with `nem` and `sem`
<!-- topics: van-nincs, focus-word-order -->
`nem` stands immediately before the word it negates, which is usually the verb: `Nem tudom.`
"I don't know." `Nem Péter jött.` "It was not Péter who came." A preverb detaches after
`nem`: `Nem megy el.` "He is not leaving."

`sem` means "neither / not … either": `Én sem tudom.` "I don't know either."
Negative pronouns take `sem` or `nem`: `Senki sem jött.` / `Senki nem jött.` "Nobody came."
`Semmit nem látok.` "I see nothing." Double negation is obligatory, as in Russian.

## Questions
<!-- topics: van-nincs, focus-word-order -->
Yes/no questions have the same word order as statements and are marked by intonation (rising
on the penultimate syllable, falling on the last): `Beszél magyarul?` "Do you speak Hungarian?"
In indirect questions and formal style the particle `-e` attaches to the verb: `Nem tudom,
hogy jön-e.` "I don't know whether he is coming."

Question words: `ki` "who", `mi` "what", `hol` "where", `hova/hová` "where to", `honnan`
"where from", `mikor` "when", `hogy/hogyan` "how", `miért` "why", `melyik` "which", `milyen`
"what kind of", `hány` "how many", `mennyi` "how much", `mennyibe kerül` "how much does it
cost". The question word normally stands directly before the verb (it is the focus):
`Hol lakik Péter?` `Mit csinálsz?`

Answering a yes/no question: `Igen.` / `Nem.`, or by repeating the verb (or the preverb):
`Jössz? — Jövök.` `Elmész? — El.`

## Personal pronouns
<!-- topics: politeness, possessive -->
| | singular | plural |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1st | `én` | `mi` |
| 2nd familiar | `te` | `ti` |
| 3rd | `ő` (he/she — no gender) | `ők` |
| 2nd polite | `ön` / `maga` | `önök` / `maguk` |

Subject pronouns are dropped unless emphasised, because the verb ending shows the person:
`Tanulok.` "I study." `Én tanulok, nem ő.` "*I* study, not him."

Pronouns are inflected by attaching the case suffix to a pronoun stem, with person endings:
accusative `engem, téged, őt, minket, titeket, őket`; dative `nekem, neked, neki, nekünk,
nektek, nekik`; `velem` "with me", `nálam` "at my place", `hozzám` "to me", `tőlem` "from me",
`rólam` "about me", `bennem` "in me", `értem` "for me". The pattern is the case suffix as a
stem plus the possessive endings (`-m, -d, -∅/-a/-e, -nk, -tok/-tek, -k`).

## `te`, `ön`, `maga` and the polite register
<!-- topics: politeness, register -->
`te` is for family, friends, children, students among themselves, and increasingly among
colleagues of similar age. `ön` (plural `önök`) is the neutral polite "you": with strangers,
officials, shop staff, in writing. `maga` (plural `maguk`) is also polite but warmer and older,
between people who know each other but are not on `te` terms; from a younger person to an older
one it can sound abrupt, so a learner is safest with `ön`. **Both `ön` and `maga` take
third-person verb forms**: `Ön beszél magyarul?` `Maguk hol laknak?`

Greetings by register: `Szia!` / `Sziasztok!` (familiar), `Jó napot kívánok!` "good day" (polite,
the universal safe greeting), `Jó reggelt!`, `Jó estét!`, `Viszontlátásra!` (polite goodbye),
`Viszlát!` (shorter, neutral), `Csókolom!` (a child to an adult, a man to an older woman).

The **`tetszik` construction** is a very polite way to address elderly people, avoiding a
direct second person: `tetszik` "it pleases (you)" plus the infinitive. `Hogy tetszik lenni?`
"How are you?" `Tetszik kérni?` "Would you like some?" `Tetszik tudni, hol van a posta?` "Do
you happen to know where the post office is?"

## Where speakers of Russian go wrong
<!-- topics: van-nincs, politeness -->
- Dropping `van` in "there is / has" sentences by analogy with Russian *у меня есть* omitted
  forms: `Van pénzem.` needs the verb; but `Péter tanár.` must not have it.
- `nem van` — the negative of `van` is `nincs`.
- Using `maga` as the default polite form because it resembles Russian *Вы*; use `ön`.
- Giving `ő` a gender: it is "he" and "she"; Hungarian has none.

# Preverbs (igekötők), word order and focus

## What a preverb is
<!-- topics: preverbs -->
A preverb is a short particle written together with the verb in its neutral position
(`elmegy` "goes away", `bejön` "comes in", `felkel` "gets up") and detached from it when
something else takes the slot before the verb. The common ones and their basic meanings:

| preverb | direction | aspect / other |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `meg-` | — | completion, result: `tanul` "studies" → `megtanul` "learns (masters)"; `ír` → `megír` "writes (finishes)" |
| `el-` | away | completion, "off": `elmegy` "leaves", `elolvas` "reads through", `elfelejt` "forgets" |
| `ki-` | out | `kimegy`, `kinyit` "opens", `kiolvas` |
| `be-` | in | `bejön`, `becsuk` "closes", `bemutat` "introduces" |
| `fel-/föl-` | up | `felkel` "gets up", `felhív` "calls (phone)", `felír` "writes down" |
| `le-` | down | `leül` "sits down", `leír` "writes down", `lefordít` "translates" |
| `át-` | across, over | `átmegy`, `átad` "hands over", `átolvas` "reads through" |
| `vissza-` | back | `visszajön`, `visszaad` "gives back" |
| `össze-` | together | `összeszed` "collects", `összetör` "breaks up" |
| `szét-` | apart | `szétszed` "takes apart" |
| `haza-` | home | `hazamegy` "goes home" |
| `ide-`, `oda-` | here, there | `idejön`, `odamegy` |
| `rá-` | onto | `rátesz` "puts on", `ránéz` "looks at" |
| `végig-` | through to the end | `végignéz` "watches through" |
| `túl-` | over, too much | `túlél` "survives", `túlbecsül` "overestimates" |

Aspect: a verb with `meg-` or `el-` usually describes a completed action, close to a Russian
perfective: `Olvastam a könyvet.` "I was reading the book." vs `Elolvastam a könyvet.` "I read
the book (to the end)." `Tanulom a magyart.` "I am learning Hungarian." vs `Megtanulom.` "I will
learn it (master it)." In the present tense a preverb verb often refers to the **future**:
`Megcsinálom.` "I'll do it." `Elmegyek.` "I'll go / I'm leaving."

## When the preverb detaches
<!-- topics: preverbs, focus-word-order -->
The preverb stays attached (before the verb) in a **neutral** sentence: `Péter elment.` "Péter
left." `Felkelek hétkor.` "I get up at seven." It moves **after the verb** when:

| trigger | example |
| --- | --- |
| a focused element stands before the verb | `Péter ment el.` "It was Péter who left." `Hétkor kelek fel.` "It's at seven that I get up." |
| a question word (always focus) | `Mikor kelsz fel?` "When do you get up?" `Hova ment el?` |
| negation with `nem` | `Nem ment el.` "He didn't leave." `Nem kelek fel.` |
| the imperative | `Menj el!` `Kelj fel!` |
| an auxiliary or a modal verb between them | `El akarok menni.` "I want to leave." `Fel kell kelnem.` "I have to get up." `El fogok menni.` "I will leave." `Ki tudom nyitni.` "I can open it." |
| a "verbal modifier" competing for the slot (a bare noun object, an adjective predicate) | `Fát vág.` "He is chopping wood." |

With the auxiliary the preverb stands **before** the auxiliary and the main verb follows in
the infinitive; under negation or focus the preverb goes after the infinitive: `Nem akarok
elmenni.` "I don't want to leave." `Péter akar elmenni.` "It is Péter who wants to leave."

The answer `Igen` can be replaced by the preverb alone: `Elmentél? — El.` "Did you leave? —
Yes." `Megcsináltad? — Meg.` "Did you do it? — Yes."

## Topic, focus and the verb
<!-- topics: focus-word-order -->
Hungarian word order is governed by information structure, not by subject–verb–object:

```
[ topic ]  [ focus ]  verb  [ the rest ]
```

- **Topic**: what the sentence is about, already known; stands first. Optional.
- **Focus**: the one element that is emphasised, new, contrasted or questioned; stands
  **immediately before the verb**, takes the main stress, and detaches the preverb. Optional.
- **Verb**: with its preverb attached if nothing is in focus.
- **The rest**: everything else, in relatively free order.

`János tegnap elvitt két könyvet Péternek.` — neutral: topic `János`, no focus, verb with
preverb, the rest.
`János tegnap két könyvet vitt el Péternek.` — focus on `két könyvet`: it was *two books* that
he took.
`Péternek vitt el János két könyvet.` — focus on `Péternek`: it was *to Péter*.
`Elvitt János két könyvet Péternek.` — verb first: emphasis on the completed action.

Words that are always focused: question words (`Ki jött el?`), negated elements (`Nem Péter
jött el.` "It wasn't Péter who came."), `csak` "only" phrases (`Csak Péter jött el.`), and
quantifiers like `kevés` "few". Words that never stand in focus: `is` "also" phrases (`Péter
is eljött.` "Péter came too." — preverb attached), `mindenki`, `minden` "every" (`Mindenki
eljött.`), which stand before the focus slot instead.

## `is`, `sem`, `csak` and negation in the structure
<!-- topics: focus-word-order -->
- `is` follows the word it belongs to and keeps the preverb attached: `Én is elmegyek.` "I'm
  going too." `Kávét is kérek.` "I'd like coffee as well."
- `sem` is `is` + negation: `Én sem megyek el.` "I'm not going either." (preverb detached,
  because the sentence is negative).
- `csak` puts its phrase in focus: `Csak kávét kérek.` "I only want coffee." (`kérek` has no
  preverb; with one: `Csak Péter ment el.`)
- Negating the verb vs negating a focus: `Nem ment el Péter.` "Péter did not leave." vs
  `Nem Péter ment el.` "It was not Péter who left (someone else did)."
- Two negatives with a negative pronoun: `Senki sem jött el.` / `Senki nem jött el.` "Nobody
  came."

## Where speakers of Russian go wrong
<!-- topics: preverbs, focus-word-order -->
- Treating the preverb as a fixed prefix like Russian *при-/у-*: it is a word that moves.
  `Nem elment` and `Mikor felkelsz?` are wrong; `Nem ment el`, `Mikor kelsz fel?`
- Putting the question word anywhere but directly before the verb: `Hol Péter lakik?` is
  wrong; `Hol lakik Péter?`
- Reading a present-tense preverb verb as present: `Elolvasom.` is "I'll read it".
- Answering with `igen` only: `El.` / `Meg.` are the idiomatic short answers.

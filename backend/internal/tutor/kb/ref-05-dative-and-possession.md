# The dative and possession

## The dative `-nak/-nek`
<!-- topics: dative -->
The dative marks the indirect object ("to/for whom"): `Adok egy könyvet Péternek.` "I give a
book to Péter." `Ezt neked hoztam.` "I brought this for you." It also appears with a set of
verbs and adjectives: `örül valaminek` "is glad of", `hisz valakinek` "believes someone",
`segít valakinek` "helps someone", `tetszik valakinek` "pleases someone" (`Tetszik nekem a
film.` "I like the film."), `kell valakinek` "someone needs", `fontos valakinek` "important
to". Pronoun forms: `nekem, neked, neki, nekünk, nektek, nekik`.

## "To have": the dative plus `van`
<!-- topics: dative, possessive -->
Hungarian has no verb "to have". Possession is `X-nak/-nek van Y-<possessive>`: literally
"to X there is his Y":

| | |
| --- | --- |
| `Van autóm.` | "I have a car." (the pronoun `nekem` is dropped) |
| `Péternek van autója.` | "Péter has a car." |
| `Nincs időm.` | "I have no time." |
| `Nekem is van kutyám.` | "I have a dog too." |
| `Van két gyerekünk.` | "We have two children." |
| `Voltak barátaid?` | "Did you have friends?" |

The possessed noun carries the possessive suffix agreeing with the possessor; the possessor,
when expressed, takes `-nak/-nek`.

## Possessive suffixes
<!-- topics: possessive -->
The possessor is marked on the possessed noun. Singular possession:

| possessor | after a vowel | after a consonant (back / front) | example: `ház`, `kert`, `autó` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| my | `-m` | `-om / -em / -öm` | `házam`, `kertem`, `autóm` |
| your (sg.) | `-d` | `-od / -ed / -öd` | `házad`, `kerted`, `autód` |
| his / her / its | `-ja / -je` | `-a / -e` or `-ja / -je` | `háza`, `kertje`, `autója` |
| our | `-nk` | `-unk / -ünk` | `házunk`, `kertünk`, `autónk` |
| your (pl.) | `-tok / -tek / -tök` | `-otok / -etek / -ötök` | `házatok`, `kertetek`, `autótok` |
| their | `-juk / -jük` | `-uk / -ük` or `-juk / -jük` | `házuk`, `kertjük`, `autójuk` |

Low-vowel stems use `-a-/-e-` linking: `házam` (not `házom`), `házatok`. Final `a/e`
lengthen: `alma` → `almám, almád, almája`. Shortening and fleeting-vowel stems change as they do
before `-t`: `kezem`, `nevem`, `bokrom`, `álmom`.

Whether the third person takes `-a/-e` or `-ja/-je` is a property of the word, not a rule.
Tendencies: after a vowel always `-ja/-je` (`autója`, `almája`, `körtéje`); after sibilants and
palatals (`c cs s sz z zs j ly ny ty gy`) the form without `j` (`háza`, `része`, `pénze`,
`lánya`, `ágya`); after other consonants either occurs (`kertje`, `padja`, `könyve`, `asztala`,
`barátja`, `orvosa`), and dictionaries list it. When in doubt, `-ja/-je` is more often right
for newer words and is understood everywhere.

## Plural possession
<!-- topics: possessive -->
Several possessed things are marked with `-i-` before the person ending, never with `-k`:

| | one thing | several things |
| --- | --- | --- |
| my | `könyvem` | `könyveim` |
| your | `könyved` | `könyveid` |
| his/her | `könyve` | `könyvei` |
| our | `könyvünk` | `könyveink` |
| your (pl.) | `könyvetek` | `könyveitek` |
| their | `könyvük` | `könyveik` |

With `ház`: `házaim, házaid, házai, házaink, házaitok, házaik`. After a vowel: `autóim,
autóid, autói, autóink, autóitok, autóik`. Stems with `-ja/-je` keep the `j`: `padjaim`,
`kertjeim`. The case suffix follows everything: `könyveimben` "in my books", `barátaimnak`
"to my friends".

## The possessive construction with a noun possessor
<!-- topics: possessive, dative -->
"Péter's book" is `Péter könyve` — the possessor unmarked, the possessed noun with the
third-person suffix. The definite article stands before the whole phrase: `a fiú könyve` "the
boy's book", `a ház ajtaja` "the door of the house". The possessor may take `-nak/-nek`, and
must when it is separated from the possessed noun or emphasised: `Péternek a könyve`,
`a fiúnak az apja` "the boy's father", `Kinek a könyve ez?` "Whose book is this?"

Plural possessor with a noun: `a fiúk könyve` "the boys' book" — the possessed noun keeps the
singular third-person suffix; `-uk/-ük` appears only when the possessor is a pronoun or is not
repeated: `az ő könyvük` "their book", `a fiúk könyvei` "the boys' books".

## The `-é` ending and possessive pronouns
<!-- topics: possessive -->
`-é` on a possessor means "that of": `Ez a könyv Péteré.` "This book is Péter's." `Kié ez?`
"Whose is this?" `A tanáré.` "The teacher's." Plural of the possessed: `-éi`: `Ezek Péteréi.`

Independent possessive pronouns: `enyém` "mine", `tied/tiéd` "yours", `övé` "his/hers",
`miénk/mienk` "ours", `tiétek/tietek` "yours", `övék` "theirs": `Ez az enyém.` "This is mine."
The emphatic possessive with a noun uses the pronoun plus the suffix: `az én házam` "my house
(as opposed to yours)", `a te könyved`, `az ő autója`, `a mi városunk`.

## Where speakers of Russian go wrong
<!-- topics: dative, possessive -->
- Marking the possessed noun with a pronoun and nothing else: `az én ház` is wrong; the suffix
  is obligatory — `a házam`, `az én házam`.
- Using the plural `-k` for several possessions: `könyvemek` does not exist; `könyveim`.
- Putting the possessor after the possessed noun as in *книга Петра*: the order is possessor
  first, `Péter könyve`.
- Confusing `-ja` "his" with `-a` "his": both are the same person; the choice is lexical.

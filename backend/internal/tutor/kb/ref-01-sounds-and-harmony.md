# Sounds, spelling and vowel harmony

## The alphabet and the digraphs
<!-- topics: orthography -->
Hungarian spelling is phonemic: one letter (or digraph) is one sound, always. The digraphs are
single letters of the alphabet and are alphabetised as such: `cs` [tʃ], `dz` [dz], `dzs` [dʒ],
`gy` [ɟ ~ "dy"], `ly` [j], `ny` [ɲ], `sz` [s], `ty` [c ~ "ty"], `zs` [ʒ].

Two traps for readers of English or Polish: `s` alone is [ʃ] ("sh") — `és` "and" is "aysh" —
while `sz` is plain [s]; and `c` alone is [ts] (`cukor` "sugar"), `cs` is [tʃ] (`csak` "only").
`j` and `ly` are both [j] today (`jó`, `hely`); the difference is historical and only matters in
spelling.

A doubled digraph doubles only its first letter: `sz`+`sz` → `ssz` (`busszal` "by bus"),
`gy`+`gy` → `ggy` (`naggyal`), `ny`+`ny` → `nny` (`könnyű`). At a line break the full form is
written (`busz-szal`).

## Vowel length is phonemic
<!-- topics: orthography -->
The seven short/long pairs are `a/á`, `e/é`, `i/í`, `o/ó`, `ö/ő`, `u/ú`, `ü/ű`. Length is not
stress and not decoration; it distinguishes words:

| short | long |
| --- | --- |
| `kor` "age, era" | `kór` "disease" |
| `öt` "five" | `őt` "him/her (acc.)" |
| `hat` "six" | `hát` "back; well…" |
| `szel` "slices" | `szél` "wind; edge" |
| `tör` "breaks" | `tőr` "dagger" |

Two pairs differ in quality as well as length: `a` is rounded and back, close to the vowel of
British "hot", while `á` is open and long like "father"; `e` is open like "bed" and `é` is
close and long, like "pay" without the glide. `o/ó`, `ö/ő`, `u/ú`, `ü/ű`, `i/í` differ in
length only. The front rounded vowels `ö ő ü ű` have no English or Russian counterpart:
`ö` is German *ö* / French *eu*, `ü` is German *ü* / French *u*.

Stress is always on the first syllable and never distinguishes words, so there is nothing to
learn about it beyond the rule.

## The harmony classes
<!-- topics: vowel-harmony -->
Almost every suffix has two or three shapes, and the vowels of the stem choose the shape.
The classes:

| class | vowels |
| --- | --- |
| back | `a á o ó u ú` |
| front unrounded | `e é i í` |
| front rounded | `ö ő ü ű` |

Two-form suffixes have a back and a front shape: `-ban/-ben`, `-nak/-nek`, `-tól/-től`,
`-val/-vel`, `-ra/-re`. Three-form suffixes distinguish the two front classes as well:
`-hoz/-hez/-höz`, `-on/-en/-ön`, `-ok/-ek/-ök` (plural after consonants), `-tok/-tek/-tök`.
A one-form suffix does not harmonise at all: `-ig` "until", `-kor` "at (time)", `-ért` "for",
`-ként` "as", `-i` (adjective), `-né` "Mrs".

| stem | class | in (`-ban/-ben`) | to (`-hoz/-hez/-höz`) | on (`-on/-en/-ön`) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `ház` "house" | back | `házban` | `házhoz` | `házon` |
| `kert` "garden" | front unrounded | `kertben` | `kerthez` | `kerten` |
| `föld` "earth" | front rounded | `földben` | `földhöz` | `földön` |

## Mixed words and the last vowel
<!-- topics: vowel-harmony -->
In a word with both back and front vowels the **last** vowel usually decides:

- last vowel back → back: `Budapest` → `Budapesten`, `vasút` "railway" → `vasúton`, `tanár` →
  `tanárnak`;
- last vowel `i`, `í` or `é` after a back vowel → still **back**, because these three are
  "neutral": `papír` → `papírban`, `kávé` "coffee" → `kávéban`, `Katalin` → `Katalinnak`,
  `Balázs` → `Balázsnak`;
- last vowel `ö`, `ő`, `ü`, `ű` → front: `sofőr` "driver" → `sofőrnek`, `sofőrhöz`;
- last vowel `e` → mostly front, with some wavering: `Ágnes` → `Ágnesnek`, `József` →
  `Józsefnek`, `hotel` → `hotelben` (also heard: `hotelban`), `fotel` → `fotelben`.

Compounds harmonise by their last member: `vasút` → `vasúton`, `hétvége` "weekend" →
`hétvégén`.

## Antiharmonic and "i" stems
<!-- topics: vowel-harmony -->
A closed list of native words with only `i`, `í` (and `é`) take **back** suffixes: `híd`
"bridge" → `hídon`, `nyíl` "arrow" → `nyílon`, `sír` "grave" → `sírba`, `ír` "writes" → `írok`,
`nyit` → `nyitok`, `bír`, `iszik` → `iszom`, `hív` → `hívok`, `cél` → `célok`, `héj` → `héja`.
Most `i`-only words are front as expected: `víz` → `vízben`, `hit` → `hitben`, `kis` → `kisebb`.
These are learned as items; there is no rule that predicts `híd` but not `hit`.

## Linking vowels
<!-- topics: vowel-harmony, plural, accusative -->
Where a consonant-final stem meets `-k` (plural), `-t` (accusative) or a possessive ending, a
linking vowel appears, and it follows harmony. The default set is `-o-/-e-/-ö-`: `pad` →
`padok, padot`; `kert` → `kertek, kertet`; `gyümölcs` → `gyümölcsök, gyümölcsöt`. Many front
rounded stems take `-e-` rather than `-ö-`: `föld` → `földek, földet`, `könyv` → `könyvek,
könyvet`. A closed group of "low-vowel" stems takes `-a-/-e-` instead: `ház` → `házak, házat`,
`toll` → `tollak, tollat`, `fog` → `fogak, fogat`, `hal` → `halak`, `fal` → `falak`, `vaj` →
`vajat`. Which stems are low-vowel is a property of the word; dictionaries mark it, and
`-a-` in the plural predicts `-a-` in the accusative and the possessive (`házam`).

Adjectives take `-a-/-e-` in the plural and accusative more often than nouns: `nagy` → `nagyok`
but `nagyot`; `piros` → `pirosak`, `pirosat`; `magas` → `magasak`.

## Where speakers of Russian go wrong
<!-- topics: orthography, vowel-harmony -->
- Reading `s` as [s]: `és`, `sok`, `város` all have [ʃ]. `sz` is the [s].
- Hearing `a` as `á` and `o` as `ó`: length is a separate contrast from quality, and `a` is
  rounder than any Russian vowel. This is a listening problem before it is a spelling one.
- Harmonising with the first vowel instead of the last: `Budapesten` (not `-on`), `kávéban`
  (`é` is neutral, so the word counts as back), `sofőrnek` (last vowel `ő`, so front).
- Stress: never move it off the first syllable; Hungarian has no Russian-style mobile stress.

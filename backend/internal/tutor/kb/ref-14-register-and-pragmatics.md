# Register, politeness and everyday pragmatics

## Choosing between `te` and `ön`
<!-- topics: politeness, register -->
Hungarian speakers mark distance by the pronoun and the verb form. `te` (informal, 2nd person
verb) is expected among students, in sports, hobby groups, most workplaces between peers, on
social media, and from adults to children; a stranger of roughly one's age in an informal
setting will often propose it (`Tegeződjünk!` "Let's use te."). `ön` (formal, 3rd person verb)
is expected with officials, doctors, older strangers, customer service in both directions, and
in written correspondence. `maga` is heard between older acquaintances and in some regions; a
learner who uses `ön` is never wrong.

A safe default in a new encounter: `ön` until the other person switches — the older or more
senior person offers the switch. Between two young adults in a bar, `te` from the start.

## Greetings, thanks and requests
<!-- topics: politeness, register -->
| situation | formal | informal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| hello | `Jó napot kívánok!` (`Jó reggelt!` before ~9, `Jó estét!` after ~18) | `Szia!` (one person), `Sziasztok!` (several); `Szervusz(tok)!` older; `Helló!` |
| goodbye | `Viszontlátásra!` / `Viszlát!` | `Szia!`, `Sziasztok!` |
| goodnight | `Jó éjszakát!` | same |
| thank you | `Köszönöm (szépen).` | `Köszi!`, `Kösz!` |
| you're welcome | `Szívesen.` `Nincs mit.` | same |
| please (offering) | `Tessék.` | `Tessék.` |
| please (asking) | `Kérem.` `Legyen szíves…` + infinitive | `Kérlek.` `Légy szíves…` |
| excuse me / sorry | `Elnézést (kérek).` `Bocsánat.` | `Bocs!` |
| how are you? | `Hogy van?` | `Hogy vagy?` `Mi újság?` |
| cheers | `Egészségére!` | `Egészségedre!` (also after a sneeze) |

`Csókolom!` is said by children to adults and by men to older women; a learner rarely needs
it. `Kezét csókolom!` is its full, old-fashioned form.

Polite requests: `Kérnék egy kávét.` "I'd like a coffee." (conditional), `Kaphatnék egy
pohár vizet?` "Could I have a glass of water?" (potential + conditional), `Legyen szíves
megmondani, hol van a kijárat.` "Would you kindly tell me where the exit is." `Meg tudná
mondani…?` "Could you tell me…?"

## Names, titles and forms of address
<!-- topics: register -->
Hungarian names are **surname first**: `Kovács Péter` — family name `Kovács`, given name
`Péter`. A married woman may be `Kovácsné` "Mrs Kovács" (`-né` on the husband's surname) or
`Kovács Péterné`, though younger women mostly keep or hyphenate their own name. Addressing
people: `uram` "sir", `asszonyom` "madam", `Kovács úr` "Mr Kovács", `Kovács asszony`; in shops
and offices simply `Elnézést!` to attract attention. Titles before the name in writing:
`Dr. Kovács Péter`; `tanár úr` / `tanárnő` "teacher (m./f.)", `doktor úr` / `doktornő`.

## Register in vocabulary and grammar
<!-- topics: register -->
- Diminutives `-ka/-ke`, `-cska/-cske`, `-i` are warm and informal: `kutyus`, `Peti`, `kicsike`,
  `sör` → `söröcske`.
- Colloquial shortening: `nem tudom` → `nem tom`; `köszönöm` → `köszi`; `szia` for both hello
  and goodbye.
- The `-e` question particle, `-ván/-vén`, the future participle and `vala`-style forms belong
  to writing; conversation uses intonation, `-va/-ve` and present-for-future.
- The definite article before a person's name (`a Péter`) is colloquial and regional; avoid it
  in writing.
- Official texts prefer `ön`, the passive-like third plural (`Kérjük, hogy…` "we ask that…"),
  and nominalisations (`a jegy megvásárlása` "the purchase of the ticket").
- Swearing and slang are heavy in speech among friends and absent from `ön` conversations; a
  learner should recognise `baszki`, `kurva` (as intensifiers) and not use them.

## Everyday conventions
<!-- topics: register -->
- Dates are written year–month–day: `2026. szeptember 2.`; the time `14.30` or `fél három`.
- Prices: `forint` (Ft, HUF); `Mennyibe kerül?` "How much is it?"; numbers are said in full,
  `ezerötszáz forint` "1,500 forints".
- On the phone: `Halló!` / `Tessék!`; introducing yourself `Kovács Péter vagyok.`
- Toasting: look the other person in the eye; beer toasts were traditionally avoided.
- Shop and office hours use `nyitva` "open" and `zárva` "closed"; `ebédszünet` "lunch break".

## Where speakers of Russian go wrong
<!-- topics: register, politeness -->
- Using `maga` as the default because it is the "vous" the textbook showed first: use `ön`.
- Calling a teacher by first name + `úr`: it is surname + `úr`, or `tanár úr` / `tanárnő`.
- Reading `Kovács Péter` as first name + surname.
- Saying `Viszontlátásra!` to a friend — `Szia!` covers both hello and goodbye informally.

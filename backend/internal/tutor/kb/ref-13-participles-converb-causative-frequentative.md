# Participles, the converb, the causative and the frequentative

## The present participle `-ó/-ő`
<!-- topics: participles -->
`-ó/-ő` (with a linking `-a-/-e-` after a cluster or `-ít`) makes an adjective meaning "…ing"
and, very often, a noun for the agent or instrument: `olvas` → `olvasó` "reading; reader",
`tanul` → `tanuló` "pupil", `ír` → `író` "writer", `vezet` → `vezető` "leader, driver", `sétál`
→ `sétáló` "walking", `ért` → `értő`, `tanít` → `tanító` "(primary) teacher", `mos` → `mosó` in
`mosógép` "washing machine", `vár` → `váró` "waiting room". Before a noun: `a síró gyerek` "the
crying child", `a következő megálló` "the next stop", `folyó víz` "running water".

## The past participle `-t/-tt`
<!-- topics: participles -->
Identical in form to the third person past: `ír` → `írt` "written", `olvas` → `olvasott`
"read", `fest` → `festett` "painted", `sül` → `sült` "fried, roast" (`sült krumpli`), `fő` →
`főtt` "boiled" (`főtt tojás`), `zár` → `zárt` "closed", `elveszít` → `elveszített` /
`elveszett` "lost". Used as an adjective: `az elolvasott könyv` "the book that has been read",
`a megírt levél` "the written letter". With `van` it is not a passive tense but a state:
`Az ajtó zárva van.` uses the converb (below); `Az ajtó zárt.` is the adjective.

## The future participle `-andó/-endő`
<!-- topics: participles -->
"To be …ed, that must be …ed": `olvasandó` "to be read", `fizetendő` "payable", `teendő` "to
be done; task", `elvégzendő munka` "work to be done", `a megoldandó feladat` "the problem to
solve". Mostly written and formal.

## The converb `-va/-ve` and `-ván/-vén`
<!-- topics: converb -->
`-va/-ve` makes an adverbial form meaning "…ing / having …ed / in a state of being …ed" —
close to the Russian деепричастие: `Sírva jött haza.` "He came home crying." `Ülve olvasok.`
"I read sitting." `Az ajtó nyitva van.` "The door is open." `Az üzlet zárva van.` "The shop is
closed." `Meg van írva.` "It is written (done)." `El van törve.` "It is broken."

The **`van` + `-va/-ve`** construction expresses a **resulting state**, not an ongoing passive
action; the preverb detaches: `Meg van csinálva.` "It has been done." It is the normal way to
say "is done / is closed / is broken". `-ván/-vén` is the literary, sequential variant:
`Megérkezvén, leült.` "Having arrived, he sat down."

The converb attaches directly to every stem, with no linking vowel: `értve`, `tanítva`,
`mondva`, `nyitva`, `festve`, `futva`.

## The causative `-at/-et`, `-tat/-tet`
<!-- topics: causative -->
The causative means "to have/make someone do something" or "to have something done":
`ír` → `írat` "has (someone) write", `olvas` → `olvastat` "makes read", `mos` → `mosat` "has
washed", `épít` → `építtet` "has built", `csinál` → `csináltat` "has made/done", `vár` → `várat`
"keeps waiting", `néz` → `nézet`, `eszik` → `etet` "feeds", `iszik` → `itat` "gives to drink",
`alszik` → `altat` "puts to sleep", `ül` → `ültet` "seats; plants", `fekszik` → `fektet` "lays
down". The short form `-at/-et` goes on one-syllable stems ending in a single consonant
(`írat`, `mosat`, `nézet`, `várat`); `-tat/-tet` goes on stems ending in `t` or in two
consonants, on `-ít` stems, and on longer stems (`olvastat`, `csináltat`, `építtet`,
`taníttat` "has taught", `fordíttat` "has translated").

The person made to act takes `-val/-vel`: `Megíratom a levelet a titkárral.` "I have the
secretary write the letter." `Levágattam a hajamat.` "I had my hair cut." (literally "I had my
hair cut off" — the thing done is the direct object.)

## The frequentative `-gat/-get`
<!-- topics: iterative -->
`-gat/-get` (with a linking vowel: `-ogat/-eget/-öget`) makes a verb repeated, prolonged,
casual or diminished: `néz` → `nézeget` "keeps looking, browses", `olvas` → `olvasgat` "reads
now and then", `ír` → `irogat` "writes from time to time", `beszél` → `beszélget` "chats",
`hall` → `hallgat` "listens" (lexicalised), `mos` → `mosogat` "washes up", `simít` → `simogat`
"strokes", `lát` → `látogat` "visits", `kér` → `kéreget` "begs", `ad` → `adogat` "keeps giving,
passes (ball)", `kopog` → `kopogat` "knocks repeatedly". Many are fully lexicalised and must be
learned as words.

## Nominalisations
<!-- topics: participles -->
`-ás/-és` makes the action noun: `tanulás` "studying", `olvasás` "reading", `érkezés`
"arrival", `kérdés` "question", `lakás` "flat" (from `lakik`), `vezetés` "driving", `mosás`
"washing". `-ó/-ő` gives the agent (`tanuló`), `-mány/-mény` the product (`tanulmány` "study,
paper", `eredmény` "result"), `-at/-et` a result (`felelet` "answer", `mondat` "sentence",
`csapat` "team").

## Where speakers of Russian go wrong
<!-- topics: participles, converb, causative -->
- Building a passive with a participle: Hungarian has no productive passive; use the third
  person plural (`Itt magyarul beszélnek.` "Hungarian is spoken here.") or the `van` +
  `-va/-ve` state.
- `Az ajtó nyitott van` — the state is `nyitva van`; `nyitott` is the adjective "open(ed)".
- Attaching the preverb to the converb construction: `Megvan csinálva` is wrong in careful
  writing; `Meg van csinálva`.
- Confusing `-at/-et` (causative) with `-hat/-het` (potential): `írat` "has written",
  `írhat` "may write".

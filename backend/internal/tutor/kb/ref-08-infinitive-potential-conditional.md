# The infinitive, the potential, and the conditional

## The infinitive `-ni`
<!-- topics: infinitive -->
The infinitive adds `-ni` to the stem: `tanulni`, `beszélni`, `ülni`, `menni` (from `megy`),
`jönni`, `lenni` (from `van`), `enni` (`eszik`), `inni` (`iszik`), `tenni`, `venni`, `hinni`,
`vinni`, `aludni`, `feküdni`. Stems ending in two consonants or in `-ít` take a linking vowel
`-a-/-e-`: `érteni` "to understand", `mondani` "to say", `tanítani` "to teach", `segíteni`,
`hallani`, `küldeni`.

The infinitive follows verbs of wanting, ability and liking: `Szeretek olvasni.` "I like
reading." `Tudok úszni.` "I can swim." `Akarok aludni.` "I want to sleep." `Megyek dolgozni.`
"I am going to work." `Kezd esni.` "It is starting to rain."

## The infinitive with personal endings
<!-- topics: infinitive -->
With the impersonal verbs `kell` "must / is necessary", `szabad` "may / is allowed", `lehet`
"can / is possible", `tilos` "is forbidden", `sikerül` "succeeds", and adjectives like `fontos`
"important", `nehéz` "hard", `jó` "good", the person who has to act is marked **on the
infinitive** — and may also appear in the dative:

| | back: `tanulni` | front: `menni` | after a cluster: `érteni` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| nekem | `tanulnom` | `mennem` | `értenem` |
| neked | `tanulnod` | `menned` | `értened` |
| neki | `tanulnia` | `mennie` | `értenie` |
| nekünk | `tanulnunk` | `mennünk` | `értenünk` |
| nektek | `tanulnotok` | `mennetek` | `értenetek` |
| nekik | `tanulniuk` | `menniük` | `érteniük` |

`Mennem kell.` "I have to go." `Nekem is dolgoznom kell.` "I have to work too." `Nem szabad
dohányoznod.` "You must not smoke." `Fontos tudnia.` "It is important for him to know."
`Sikerült megtalálnunk.` "We managed to find it." Without a person the plain infinitive
states a general obligation: `Itt nem szabad parkolni.` "Parking is not allowed here."
`Tanulni kell.` "One has to study."

`kell` takes a **noun in the nominative** when what is needed is a thing: `Kell egy toll.` "A
pen is needed." `Nekem kell a könyv.` "I need the book." Its past is `kellett`, its conditional
`kellene` (`Mennem kellene.` "I ought to go.").

## The potential `-hat/-het`
<!-- topics: potential -->
`-hat/-het` after the stem means "can, may, is allowed to, might", and the result is conjugated
like any verb, in both conjugations and every tense: `tanulhatok` "I may study", `mehetek` "I
can go", `jöhetsz` "you can come", `láthatja` "he can see it", `ehetünk` "we can eat",
`ihatnak` "they can drink". Irregular stems use the short stem: `megy` → `mehet`, `jön` →
`jöhet`, `van` → `lehet`, `tesz` → `tehet`, `vesz` → `vehet`, `eszik` → `ehet`, `iszik` →
`ihat`, `alszik` → `alhat`, `fekszik` → `fekhet`.

Past: `tanulhattam` "I could have studied / was able to study", `mehettünk` "we could go".
Conditional: `tanulhatnék` "I could study (if…)", `elmehetnénk` "we could go".

It differs from `tud` "knows how to, is able to": `Tudok úszni.` "I can swim (I have the
skill)." vs `Úszhatok?` "May I swim?" and from `lehet` "it is possible": `Lehet, hogy jön.`
"He may come." A polite request: `Kaphatok egy kávét?` "Could I have a coffee?"

## The conditional
<!-- topics: conditional -->
The conditional marker is `-na/-ne` (`-ná/-né` in some persons), placed before the person
ending. Indefinite:

| | `tanul` | `kér` | `ül` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| én | `tanulnék` | `kérnék` | `ülnék` |
| te | `tanulnál` | `kérnél` | `ülnél` |
| ő | `tanulna` | `kérne` | `ülne` |
| mi | `tanulnánk` | `kérnénk` | `ülnénk` |
| ti | `tanulnátok` | `kérnétek` | `ülnétek` |
| ők | `tanulnának` | `kérnének` | `ülnének` |

Note the first person singular: **`-nék` for every stem**, back stems included (`tanulnék`,
`várnék`, `mondanék`) — the one place where a back-vowel verb takes a front ending.

Definite:

| | `tanul` | `kér` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| én | `tanulnám` | `kérném` |
| te | `tanulnád` | `kérnéd` |
| ő | `tanulná` | `kérné` |
| mi | `tanulnánk` | `kérnénk` |
| ti | `tanulnátok` | `kérnétek` |
| ők | `tanulnák` | `kérnék` |

Stems ending in two consonants or `-ít` take the linking vowel: `értenék`, `mondanám`,
`tanítanál`. Irregular: `mennék, mennél, menne`; `jönnék, jönnél, jönne`; `lennék, lenne`
(from `van`/`lesz`); `tennék`; `vennék`; `ennék, enne`; `innék, inna` (back!); `aludnék,
aludna`; `feküdnék`.

The **past conditional** is the past tense plus the particle `volna`, which stays in that
form for every person: `tanultam volna` "I would have studied", `elmentél volna` "you would
have gone", `ha tudtam volna` "if I had known".

Uses: polite wishes and requests (`Szeretnék egy kávét.` "I would like a coffee." `Kérnék
egy jegyet.`), conditions (`Ha lenne időm, olvasnék.` "If I had time, I would read."),
and softened statements (`Azt mondanám, hogy…`).

## Where speakers of Russian go wrong
<!-- topics: infinitive, potential, conditional -->
- Building "I have to" with a personal verb: Hungarian says `mennem kell` "to-go-my is
  necessary", never `kellek menni`.
- `tanulnák` for "I would study": the indefinite is `tanulnék`; `tanulnák` is "they would
  study it" (definite, 3rd plural).
- Using `tud` for permission: `Bejöhetek?` "May I come in?", not `Be tudok jönni?` (which asks
  about physical ability).
- Forming a past conditional with a separate word as in *бы*: `volna` follows the past-tense
  verb and does not move.
